# Late TDS Return and Correction Guide: Section 234E Fees, Form 26Q & TRACES Steps

For finance controllers, accounts teams, and business owners in India, deducting Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) and depositing it via Challan ITNS 281 is only the first half of compliance. 

The second half is compiling and filing the quarterly TDS statements—**Form 24Q (Salary)**, **Form 26Q (Resident Non-Salary)**, and **Form 27Q (Non-Resident Remittances)**.

If a quarterly TDS statement is filed after the statutory cutoff date, or if it is submitted with typos in a contractor's PAN, mismatched challan numbers, or incorrect deduction rates, the consequences are immediate:
- The deductor is hit with a non-negotiable **₹200 per day late fee under Section 234E**.
- Your vendors and employees cannot see their tax credits in **Form 26AS**, blocking their personal ITR filings and damaging business relationships.
- CPC-TDS issues automated **Intimations under Section 200A** demanding immediate interest and penalty settlements.

This guide explains the quarterly compliance calendar, fee limits, and how to fix errors online via the **TRACES portal**.

---

## Quarterly Due Dates for TDS Return Filing

Under Section 200(3) read with Rule 31A of the Income Tax Rules, quarterly TDS statements must be filed on or before the following statutory deadlines:

| Quarter | Period Covered | Statutory Filing Due Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | April 1 to June 30 | **July 31** |
| **Q2** | July 1 to September 30 | **October 31** |
| **Q3** | October 1 to December 31 | **January 31** |
| **Q4** | January 1 to March 31 | **May 31** (Extended 60-day window for year-end reconciliations) |

*Note: For government deductors depositing tax without challan (book entry), returns are due on the 15th of the month following the quarter.*

---

## Section 234E: The Mandatory ₹200 Daily Late Fee

Section 234E imposes a mandatory statutory late fee for every single day a TDS statement remains overdue:

$$\text{Late Fee} = ₹200 \times \text{Number of Days Delayed}$$

### Key Legal Rules Governing Section 234E
1. **The Maximum Cap:** Under Section 234E(2), the total late fee **cannot exceed the total amount of TDS deductible/collectible** in that return. If your total TDS for the quarter was ₹12,000, your maximum fee is capped at ₹12,000, even if the filing was delayed by a year.
2. **Mandatory, Not Discretionary:** The assessing officer cannot waive or reduce Section 234E fees under any discretionary powers.
3. **Must Be Paid Before Filing:** The late fee must be calculated and deposited via Challan ITNS 281 under the "Fee under Section 234E" minor head before the return will be accepted by the portal.

---

## Section 271H / Section 461: Substantial Penalties & The One-Month Safe Harbor Relief

In addition to the daily ₹200 fee under Section 234E, failure to file prescribed TDS/TCS statements attracts a substantial penalty under **Section 271H of the Income-tax Act, 1961** (transitioning to **Section 461 of the Income Tax Act, 2025** for failure to furnish statements, distinguishing from Section 508 which governs statements of financial transactions/reportable accounts):
- **Penalty Range:** Minimum **₹10,000** up to a maximum of **₹1,00,000** (under Section 271H of the 1961 Act and Section 461 of the 2025 Act).
- **Applicability:** Levied for failure to submit quarterly returns/statements on time, or for furnishing incorrect information (such as fake PANs or falsified challans).

### The Statutory Safe Harbor (Section 271H(2) & Section 461 Relief)
To protect bona fide businesses that experience operational delays, Section 271H(2) of the 1961 Act and Section 461 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 provide complete immunity from this penalty if the deductor proves that:
1. The total tax deducted or collected has been fully paid to the credit of the Central Government;
2. The late fee under Section 234E and interest under Section 201(1A) have been fully settled; AND
3. The quarterly statement is **filed within one month from the prescribed statutory due date** (amended with effect from Assessment Year 2025-26; under prior law, the relief window was one year).

## Common Errors That Require a TDS Correction Statement

After filing an original TDS return, CPC-TDS generates an **Intimation under Section 200A** highlighting processing discrepancies. The most frequent errors include:

1. **Invalid or Inoperative Deductee PAN (Short Deduction):** If a vendor's PAN was mistyped or was inoperative due to non-linkage with Aadhaar, tax is required to be deducted at **20% under Section 206AA**. If you deducted standard 1% or 2%, the system flags a massive short-deduction demand.
2. **Challan Over-Allocation or Mismatch:** An error in the BSR code, challan date, or challan serial number prevents the return from consuming the deposited tax.
3. **Omission of Deductees:** A vendor invoice paid during the quarter was completely left out of the original filing.
4. **Incorrect Section Code:** Categorizing a technical fee under Section 194C instead of Section 194J.

---

> **Statutory Period & Transition Context (AY 2026-27 vs Tax Year 2026-27):**  
> Quarterly TDS returns and correction statements for transactions executed up to March 31, 2026 (FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27) are governed by Sections 200(3), 200A, 234E, and 271H of the Income-tax Act, 1961. For transactions executed on or after April 1, 2026, withholding and reporting compliance transition to the corresponding withholding schedule under the Income Tax Act, 2025, where the penalty for failure to furnish prescribed statements is governed by Section 461 (retaining the one-month statutory relief condition).

## The Six Categories of TRACES Correction Statements

CPC-TDS classifies online and offline correction statements into structured categories:

| Category Code | Scope of Correction | Permitted Platform |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Pan-India correction of Deductor personal details or address | Online TRACES / NSDL |
| **C2** | Correction of Challan details (BSR code, date, serial number) | Online TRACES / Offline RPU |
| **C3** | Correction of Deductee details (PAN, name, or record update) | Online TRACES / Offline RPU |
| **C4** | Correction of Salary details (Form 24Q Annexure II) | Offline RPU |
| **C5** | Addition or deletion of fresh deductee records | Online TRACES / Offline RPU |
| **C9** | Addition of a completely new unconsumed Challan | Online TRACES |

## Step-by-Step Guide to Filing an Online Correction on TRACES

For routine PAN updates, challan matching, and add/delete deductees, you do not need to download the complex Return Preparation Utility (RPU). You can complete the correction **directly inside your web browser on TRACES**:

1. **Log In:** Access `tdscpc.gov.in` as a Deductor using your TAN and credentials.
2. **Initiate Request:** Navigate to **Defaults > Request for Correction**.
3. **Select Period:** Choose the relevant Financial Year, Quarter, and Form Type (e.g., Form 26Q).
4. **Choose Mode:** Select category **"Online"** and submit the request.
5. **Complete KYC:** Enter the 15-digit Token / PRN number of the original return, along with BSR code, challan date, serial number, and 3 deductee voucher amounts for validation.
6. **Edit Records:** Access the Online Correction Module to update invalid PANs or allocate unconsumed challans.
7. **Digitally Sign:** Submit the correction using an authorized signatory's Class 3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC).
8. **CPC Processing:** CPC-TDS processes the online correction within 24 to 72 hours, wiping out short-deduction defaults under Section 200A.

### Authentication (KYC) Requirements
To unlock the correction module, TRACES requires token validation:
- **Token Number / PRN:** The 15-digit Provisional Receipt Number of the original return.
- **Challan Validation:** BSR code, challan date, challan serial number, and total tax amount from one matched challan in that return.
- **Deductee Validation:** Three PAN numbers and corresponding voucher amounts matched against that challan.

## How to Correct Invalid PANs and Overcome Short-Deduction Demands

When an intimation under Section 200A creates an unexpected demand for 20% tax under Section 206AA:
1. Request the vendor to furnish their correct, active PAN card.
2. Initiate an **Online Correction (C3)** on TRACES.
3. Replace the invalid PAN with the verified PAN.
4. If the new PAN is active and operative, the algorithm automatically re-rates the transaction to the original 1%, 2%, or 10% rate.
5. The 20% short-deduction demand is **wiped out**, and the revised TDS credit appears in the vendor's Form 26AS within 48 hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can the Section 234E late filing fee be waived by the Assessing Officer?
No. Section 234E is a mandatory statutory fee, not a discretionary penalty. Indian courts have upheld that the CPC system automatically calculates ₹200 per day, and Assessing Officers have no statutory power to waive it once triggered.

### What is the maximum limit of late fees under Section 234E?
The total late fee under Section 234E cannot exceed the total amount of tax deductible or collectible for the relevant quarter. For example, if total TDS in a return was ₹15,000, the late fee cannot exceed ₹15,000, regardless of the number of days delayed.

### What is a TRACES 'Online Correction' vs an 'Offline Correction'?
An Online Correction allows the deductor to fix PAN errors, unconsumed challans, and deductee rows directly within the web browser on `tdscpc.gov.in` using DSC. An Offline Correction requires downloading a Conso File, editing it via the NSDL RPU tool, generating a `.fvu` file, and submitting it through an e-TDS TIN-FC center or the portal.

### How long does it take for a TRACES correction statement to be processed?
Online corrections on TRACES are typically processed by CPC-TDS within 24 to 72 hours. Once processed, the revised TDS credits reflect in the deductee's Form 26AS, wiping out prior short-deduction or mismatch notices.

### What is the statutory safe harbor window under Section 271H(2) and Section 461 to avoid penalty?
Under Section 271H(2) of the 1961 Act (and corresponding Section 461 of the Income Tax Act, 2025), penalty between ₹10,000 and ₹1,00,000 is not levied if the deductor proves that tax deducted/collected, Section 201(1A) interest, and Section 234E late fees have been deposited, and the quarterly TDS statement is filed within one month from the prescribed statutory due date (applicable w.e.f. AY 2025-26; replacing the earlier one-year period).
