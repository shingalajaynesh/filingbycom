# ROC Annual Filings Triad: Form ADT-1, AOC-4 and MGT-7/7A Compliance Guide

Incorporating a Private Limited Company provides founders with limited liability, separate legal personality, and an institutional framework to raise venture capital. However, corporate status also brings rigorous statutory transparency obligations under the **Companies Act, 2013**.

Regardless of whether your company generated ₹10 crore in revenue or remained completely dormant without a single commercial invoice, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) mandates the annual submission of three vital e-forms:
1. **Form ADT-1:** Appointment of Statutory Auditors (Section 139).
2. **Form AOC-4:** Submission of Audited Financial Statements and Director's Report (Section 137).
3. **Form MGT-7 / MGT-7A:** Submission of the Corporate Annual Return of shareholding and governance (Section 92).

Failing to complete this annual triad on the **MCA V3 portal** leads to severe operational consequences: immediate late fees of **₹100 per day per form**, freezing of director DINs, disqualification under Section 164(2), and the risk of company strike-off by the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

---

## Form ADT-1: Statutory Auditor Appointment (Section 139)

Section 139 mandates that every company must have an appointed statutory auditor to inspect its financial books:

- **First Auditor:** Appointed by the Board of Directors within **30 days** of incorporation via Board Resolution. While filing Form ADT-1 for the first auditor was technically optional in older rules, filing Form ADT-1 on MCA V3 has become best practice to establish a clean audit trail.
- **Subsequent Auditor (5-Year Tenure):** At the first Annual General Meeting (AGM), shareholders officially appoint the statutory auditor (an individual CA or firm) to hold office from the conclusion of that AGM until the conclusion of the **6th AGM** (a 5-year tenure).
- **Filing Window:** Form ADT-1 must be filed with the ROC within **15 days of the AGM** at which the appointment was made.
- **Key Attachments:**
  1. Written consent letter from the auditor in Form ADT-1 format.
  2. Eligibility certificate from the auditor under Section 141 confirming they are not disqualified.
  3. Certified true copy of the Ordinary Resolution passed at the AGM.

---

## Form AOC-4: Filing Financial Statements (Section 137)

Form AOC-4 is the official vehicle through which a company presents its annual audited financial accounts to the Registrar:

1. **Auditor Completes Statutory Company Audit & Issues Audit Report**
2. **Board Approves Financial Statements & Director's Report**
3. **Shareholders Adopt Accounts at Annual General Meeting (AGM)**
4. **File Form AOC-4 on MCA V3 Within 30 Days of AGM with Attachments**

### Mandatory Attachments for Form AOC-4
1. **Balance Sheet and Profit & Loss Account** with all notes to accounts.
2. **Auditor's Report:** Duly signed with active **Unique Document Identification Number (UDIN)**.
3. **Director's Report:** Prepared in accordance with Section 134, detailing corporate operations, conservation of energy, technology absorption, foreign exchange earnings, and risk management policies.
4. **Form AOC-2:** Disclosure of contracts or arrangements entered into with related parties under Section 188.
5. **Notice of AGM:** Copy of the official notice convening the annual general meeting.

## Form MGT-7 vs MGT-7A: Annual Return of Capital & Shareholding

Under Section 92, every company must submit an annual snapshot of its corporate structure, capital capitalization, and management composition:

| Parameter | Form MGT-7 (Standard Return) | Form MGT-7A (Abridged Return) |
|---|---|---|
| **Applicability** | Medium and large Private Limited Companies, Public Companies | **Small Companies** and **One Person Companies (OPCs)** exclusively |
| **Small Company Threshold** | Paid-up Capital > ₹4 Crore OR Turnover > ₹40 Crore | Paid-up Capital $\le$ **₹4 Crore** AND Turnover $\le$ **₹40 Crore** |
| **Certification Requirement** | Mandatory digital certification by an independent practicing Company Secretary (PCS) | Self-certified by directors; no PCS certification required |
| **Filing Due Date** | Within **60 days of the AGM** | Within **60 days of the AGM** |

### What is Disclosed in the Annual Return?
- Registered office address and principal business activities.
- Authorized, subscribed, and paid-up share capital.
- Complete register of members and debenture holders (including transfers during the year).
- Promoters, directors, and key managerial personnel (KMP) details.
- Number of Board meetings and Committee meetings held during the year, alongside attendance records.
- Details of legal remuneration paid to directors.

## Master Timeline: AGM and the 15/30/60 Day Clocks

The statutory compliance calendar is anchored to the date of the **Annual General Meeting (AGM)**:

- **Statutory AGM Deadline:** Under Section 96, a company must hold its AGM within **6 months from the close of the financial year** (i.e., on or before **September 30**).
- For a financial year closing on March 31, if the AGM is held on September 25:
  1. **Form ADT-1 (15 Days):** Due by **October 10**.
  2. **Form AOC-4 (30 Days):** Due by **October 25**.
  3. **Form MGT-7 / MGT-7A (60 Days):** Due by **November 24**.

---

## The ₹100 Per Day Compounding Penalty (Section 403)

Under Section 403 read with the Companies (Registration Offices and Fees) Rules, 2014, the penalty for delayed ROC filing is non-negotiable:

$$\text{Additional Late Fee} = ₹100 \times \text{Number of Days Delayed} \quad (\text{Per Form})$$

### The Math of Procrastination
- If a company delays filing both **Form AOC-4** and **Form MGT-7** by just 60 days:
  - AOC-4 Additional Fee: $60 \times 100 = ₹6,000$
  - MGT-7 Additional Fee: $60 \times 100 = ₹6,000$
  - **Total Additional Penalty:** **₹12,000** (over and above standard nominal filing fees).
- **No Statutory Ceiling:** Unlike direct tax late fees that have maximum caps, the ₹100/day penalty accumulates indefinitely until the form is cleared on MCA V3.

## Small Company Benefits: Exemption from Cash Flow & Secretarial Audit

Recognizing that early-stage ventures need lean compliance, Section 2(85) provides significant exemptions to **"Small Companies"** (Paid-up capital $\le$ ₹4 crore and turnover $\le$ ₹40 crore):
1. **No Cash Flow Statement:** A small company is statutorily exempt from preparing and attaching a Cash Flow Statement in Form AOC-4.
2. **Simplified Annual Return:** Files the lightweight **Form MGT-7A** instead of the extensive Form MGT-7.
3. **No PCS Certification:** Directors can sign the return directly without hiring a practicing Company Secretary.
4. **Fewer Board Meetings:** Required to hold only **two Board meetings per year** (one in each half of the calendar year with a minimum gap of 90 days), rather than four.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a private limited company with zero turnover skip filing AOC-4 and MGT-7?
No. Filing AOC-4 and MGT-7 is a mandatory statutory obligation for every incorporated company under the Companies Act 2013, regardless of revenue or commercial activity. Inactive companies must file nil returns or officially apply for Dormant Status under Section 455.

### What is the difference between Form MGT-7 and Form MGT-7A?
Form MGT-7 is the standard Annual Return for medium and large companies, requiring certification by a practicing Company Secretary. Form MGT-7A is an abridged, simplified Annual Return designed exclusively for One Person Companies (OPCs) and Small Companies, requiring only director sign-off.

### What happens if the company fails to hold an AGM within the statutory period?
If an AGM is not held by September 30, the company must still file AOC-4 and MGT-7 within 30 and 60 days of the due date, noting the reasons for non-holding. Failing to hold an AGM attracts penalties under Section 99.

### Who can digitally sign Form AOC-4 and MGT-7?
Form AOC-4 must be digitally signed by at least one director (using DSC) and certified by a practicing Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary, or Cost Accountant. Form MGT-7A requires digital signature by a director.
