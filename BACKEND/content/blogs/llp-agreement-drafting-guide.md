# LLP Agreement Drafting Guide: Essential Clauses, Stamp Duty & Form 3 Filing

The **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) Agreement** serves as the supreme constitutional charter of an LLP in India. While a Private Limited Company is strictly governed by statutory Articles of Association (AoA) and the formal procedural mechanics of the Companies Act, an LLP enjoys structural flexibility. Under **Section 23 of the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008**, the mutual rights, duties, capital obligations, and operational governance of the partners are governed almost entirely by their written contract.

However, contractual flexibility is a double-edged sword. A poorly drafted partnership deed—often copied from generic internet templates—inevitably creates catastrophic shareholder deadlock, tax disallowances, or expensive disputes during partner exits.

This comprehensive guide details the essential clauses, tax structuring requirements under **Section 40(b) of the Income Tax Act**, state-specific stamp duty considerations, and the statutory procedure for filing **Form 3** with the Registrar of Companies (ROC).

---

## Statutory Framework Under the LLP Act, 2008

Under corporate law, an LLP is a separate legal entity distinct from its partners. The foundational architecture governing LLP agreements includes:

1. **Section 23(1)**: The mutual rights and duties of the partners of an LLP, and the mutual rights and duties of the LLP and its partners, shall be governed by the LLP agreement.
2. **Section 23(2)**: The LLP agreement and any changes made therein must be filed with the Registrar of Companies in **Form 3** within **30 days** of incorporation or amendment.
3. **Section 23(4)**: In the absence of an agreement as to any matter, the mutual rights and duties of the partners and the mutual rights and duties of the LLP and the partners shall be determined by the provisions relating to that matter as set out in the **First Schedule** of the Act.

---

## The Danger of Relying on the First Schedule Default Rules

Many founders fail to realize that if their LLP Agreement remains silent on a specific operational issue, the **First Schedule of the LLP Act, 2008** automatically steps in as the default legal framework. 

Relying on these default provisions can produce disastrous business consequences:

### FIRST SCHEDULE DEFAULTS VS. TAILORED DRAFTING

| **First Schedule Statutory Default** | **Customized LLP Agreement Provision** |
| --- | --- |
| 1. All partners share profits | Profits can be tied to performance, |
| and capital strictly EQUALLY. | equity contribution, or time spent. |
| 2. NO partner is entitled to | Working partners receive structured |

| 3. Every partner can participate | Operational powers can be delegated |
| in everyday management. | exclusively to Designated Partners. |

## Core Operational Clauses Every LLP Agreement Must Include

To provide absolute legal certainty, an LLP Agreement must contain robustly drafted provisions covering the entire operational lifecycle:

### 1. Capital Contribution and Maintenance of Capital Accounts
* **Initial Contribution**: Specify whether contributions are made in cash, tangible property, or intangible assets (intellectual property, code, client contracts) with verified valuation reports.
* **Future Capital Calls**: Define whether partners are obligated to infuse additional working capital upon formal demand, and specify the dilution penalties if a partner fails to meet a capital call.

### 2. Profit and Loss Allocation
* Explicitly state the exact percentage split for net divisible profits and business losses. 
* Clarify that losses will be absorbed up to the limit of each partner’s agreed capital contribution, protecting individual personal wealth against entity-level liabilities under Section 27.

### 3. Management Powers & Decision-Making Tiers
* Establish a clear demarcation between **Ordinary Business Decisions** (resolved by a simple majority vote) and **Reserved / Supermajority Matters** (requiring 75% or 100% written consent).
* Reserved matters should always include: altering bank sign-off thresholds, taking on external debt above pre-set limits, admitting a new partner, opening branch offices, or entering into related-party contracts.

### 4. Cheque-Signing & Banking Limits
* Establish dual-signatory mandates for expenditures exceeding a specific threshold (e.g., transactions above ₹1,00,000 require joint digital/physical approval from two Designated Partners).

## Structuring Partner Remuneration & Interest Under Section 40(b)

From an income tax perspective, an LLP is taxed at a flat base rate of **30%** (plus applicable surcharge and health & education cess). Unlike corporate dividends, profit distributed to partners after entity-level tax is completely exempt in the hands of the partners under **Section 10(2A) of the Income Tax Act**.

However, paying partner salaries and interest on capital allows the LLP to reduce its net taxable book profit. Under **Section 40(b)**, these deductions are allowed **only if the following conditions are met**:

1. **Statutory Condition 1: Express Provision in Agreement**
2. **Statutory Condition 2: Eligible Working Partners Only**
3. **Statutory Condition 3: Cap on Interest on Capital**
4. **Statutory Condition 4: Section 40(b) Remuneration Slabs on Book Profit**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If your LLP Agreement merely states "remuneration shall be decided mutually by partners from time to time," the Assessing Officer will **disallow the entire salary deduction**, resulting in a 30% tax penalty plus interest on the disallowed amount. The agreement must contain an enforceable mathematical formula or defined schedule.

## State-Wise Stamp Duty & Execution Protocols

Under the **Indian Stamp Act, 1899** and state-specific stamp enactments, an LLP Agreement is an instrument that must be executed on non-judicial stamp paper of appropriate value. 

Because stamp duty is a state subject, rates vary dramatically across jurisdictions based on total capital contribution:

| State | Applicable Stamp Duty Framework | Indicative Rate for ₹1,00,000 Capital |
|---|---|---|
| **Maharashtra** | Maharashtra Stamp Act, Art 47 | 0.1% of capital contribution (Min ₹500, Max ₹15,000) |
| **Karnataka** | Karnataka Stamp Act, Art 40 | Slabs starting at ₹1,000 for capital up to ₹10 Lakhs |
| **Delhi** | Indian Stamp Act (Delhi Schedule) | Fixed duty of ₹1,000 or 1% based on capital band |
| **Gujarat** | Gujarat Stamp Act, Art 44 | 1% of total capital contribution (Max ₹10,000) |
| **Uttar Pradesh** | UP Stamp Act, Art 46 | ₹750 fixed plus additional tiered stamp duty |
| **Tamil Nadu** | Tamil Nadu Stamp Act | Fixed duty of ₹300 to ₹1,000 depending on capital structure |

### Execution Protocol:
1. Print the finalized legal text on the non-judicial stamp paper purchased in the state where the registered office is located.
2. Every partner must sign every page of the agreement.
3. Two independent witnesses must affix their full signatures, names, and residential addresses on the execution page.
4. Obtain notarization from a licensed Notary Public.

## Filing Form 3 on MCA V3: Timelines & Late Penalties

Once executed and stamped, the agreement must be submitted to the Ministry of Corporate Affairs:

1. **Incorporate LLP via FiLLiP**
2. **Execute Agreement on Non-Judicial Stamp Paper & Notarise**
3. **Draft Form 3 Webform on MCA V3 Portal**
4. **Attach Scanned Copy of Stamped Agreement + PAN + Consents**
5. **Affix Class 3 DSC of Designated Partner + Practising CA/CS/CMA**
6. **Pay Government ROC Fee & Obtain Filing SRN**

### Consequences of Delayed Filing:
Under the Companies and LLP framework, missing the 30-day window triggers continuous daily additional fees. In an operational LLP, unfiled agreements prevent any subsequent modifications to capital, partner composition, or banking mandates.

## Drafting for Future Exits, Buyouts, and Dispute Resolution

To safeguard long-term continuity, an LLP Agreement must provide clear exits:

### 1. Voluntary Retirement and Notice Periods
Require an outgoing partner to serve at least **30 to 90 days of written notice**. Specify how their capital and accumulated profits will be repaid (e.g., quarterly installments over 12 months to prevent cash flow depletion).

### 2. Fair Market Valuation Formula
Avoid valuation disputes by establishing a pre-agreed valuation methodology—such as Net Asset Value (NAV) or Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) conducted by an independent Registered Valuer.

### 3. Non-Compete and Confidentiality Restraints
Subject to Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, include enforceable non-solicitation clauses preventing departing partners from poaching employees, intellectual assets, or existing clients for a defined period.

### 4. Dispute Resolution Mechanism
Mandate that all partner disputes must first undergo 30 days of formal mediation. If mediation fails, the matter must be referred to sole arbitration under the **Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996**, with the seat of arbitration fixed in the city of the registered office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if an LLP fails to file Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation?
Under Section 23(2) of the LLP Act read with Rule 21, the executed agreement must be filed in Form 3 within 30 days of incorporation. Missing the deadline attracts daily compounding additional government filing fees until the form is cleared on the MCA V3 portal.

### Can working partners receive a salary if it is not mentioned in the LLP Agreement?
No. Under the First Schedule of the LLP Act, no partner is entitled to remuneration for acting in the business. Furthermore, under Section 40(b) of the Income Tax Act, partner remuneration is strictly non-deductible as an expense unless explicitly authorized by the partnership deed.

### How is stamp duty on an LLP Agreement calculated across Indian states?
Stamp duty is governed by respective State Stamp Acts and is usually pegged to the total capital contribution of the LLP. For example, Maharashtra levies 0.1% of capital (subject to minimum and maximum caps), Delhi charges on fixed slabs or 1%, and Karnataka levies tiered duties starting at ₹1,000.

### Can an LLP Agreement be altered after initial ROC approval?
Yes. Whenever partners amend capital contributions, profit ratios, or management powers, they must execute a Supplementary LLP Agreement on appropriate non-judicial stamp paper and file Form 3 with the ROC within 30 days of execution.

### Is notarization of the LLP Agreement mandatory before uploading Form 3?
While the LLP Act requires execution on non-judicial stamp paper with partner signatures and two witness attestations, most ROC jurisdictions mandate notarization before an advocate or notary public to verify authenticity upon MCA V3 submission.
